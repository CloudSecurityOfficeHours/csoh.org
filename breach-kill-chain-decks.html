Breach Kill Chain Slide Decks

Each deck turns one of the site's breach kill chains into slides you can read on your own or present to a room, with a presenter's script in the speaker notes. Copy one, cut it to fit your slot, and add your own environment to it.

Every deck is built from one kill chain page on this site: the same stages, the same MITRE ATT&CK techniques, the same figures and the same sources. A script checks each deck's figures, techniques and sources against its page, and each deck is reviewed against the page before it is published. The slides are free to share and adapt for non-commercial use under CC BY-NC 4.0.

Decks are published in batches, newest kill chains first. The list below grows as each one passes its checks, and the kill chain page links its deck at the same time.

What every deck contains

The decks share one shape, so once you have presented one you can find your way around the others. A section is left out when its kill chain has nothing to put in it.

  1. A cover giving the date of the kill chain the deck reflects, then a slide on how to use the deck.
  2. At a glance: the headline figures, each with who reported it.
  3. What happened, with a timeline when the page gives dates, and a slide on who reported what when the sources disagree.
  4. The whole kill chain on one slide: every stage with its phase and its ATT&CK technique.
  5. One slide per stage, with its techniques, its key facts and why the stage matters.
  6. Background slides for concepts a stage relies on, such as XML external entities or IMDSv2. Each is labelled Background and names its own source, so you can tell general explanation from the facts of the incident.
  7. Defenses, starting with a matrix of which defense breaks which stage. A stage that no defense on the page addresses keeps an empty column, on purpose.
  8. Hunting markers, where the page gives them, with the page's caveats about how quickly they change.
  9. Check your own environment: the page's defenses turned into questions about your own estate.
  10. Discussion questions, each followed by an answer slide.
  11. Sources, related kill chains, and a closing slide that links the full page.

Every slide between the how-to slide and the closing slide has speaker notes written as a script for the presenter. The notes add narration and detail, and they never state a fact the slides contradict.

Presenting a deck

The notes are your script, so start there. Make your own copy (see below), open it in Google Slides and read the notes under each slide once through before you present. When you present, open the menu beside the Slideshow button and choose Presenter view: the room sees the slides and you see the notes.

Say the attributions out loud. A figure on a slide comes with its source ("per CrowdStrike") and its hedge ("about", "moderate-confidence"), and the notes repeat both. Dropped in the telling, a vendor's estimate becomes a fact you asserted.

Fit it to your slot

  • For a short slot, like the 10 to 15 minute lightning format at the Friday session, keep the cover, At a glance, the kill chain on one slide, the stage slides and one discussion question.
  • For a full session, present the whole deck, and decide first how much time the discussion questions get. That is where the room's own experience comes out.
  • For a room that already knows a concept, skip its Background slide. In your copy, right-click the slide in the filmstrip and choose Skip slide: it stays in the deck, and the slideshow passes over it.

Run the discussion

Each discussion question has its answer on the next slide. Stop on the question, let the room argue, then move on to the answer, which says what the kill chain page says and gives the discussion somewhere to land. The defense matrix makes a good extra prompt: where a stage has an empty column, the page offers no defense for it, so ask the room what they would add.

Use it for team training

Run the Check your own environment slide as a working session. Each line on it is a question about your own estate, such as whether your instances require IMDSv2, and the questions nobody can answer become a short to-do list. A deck makes a ready-made opening for a tabletop exercise too: walk the stages, and at each one ask what your logs would have shown and who would have been paged.

Make it your own

The Make your own copy link asks you to sign in to a Google account, then saves an editable copy of the deck to your Google Drive. Edits stay in your copy and never touch the original. To work in PowerPoint or Keynote, open your copy and choose File, then Download, then Microsoft PowerPoint (.pptx).

Some ways teams adapt a deck:

  • After each stage slide, add one showing how that step would look in your accounts: the log that would record it, the alert that would fire and the team that would respond.
  • Where the deck lists the page's hunting markers, add the queries your team runs.
  • Pair two decks that share an actor or a technique; where a deck has a Related kill chains slide, it names them.
  • Cut the deck to the time you have, translate it, or move it onto your own slide template.

What the license asks

The decks, and the plain-text outlines they are built from, are licensed CC BY-NC 4.0. In short, you may share and adapt them for non-commercial purposes if you:

  • keep the credit to Cloud Security Office Hours, csoh.org, which the cover and closing slides carry;
  • link the license; and
  • say that you changed the deck, for example with "Adapted from csoh.org, with changes" on your cover.

Non-commercial means not primarily intended for commercial advantage or payment, so selling the slides, or charging for a course built on them, falls outside the license. Material a slide quotes from a third party keeps its own license. The license text is the authority here; this section is a summary of it, not legal advice.

Keep the hedges when you edit

Every figure in a deck says who reported it and how sure they are, because the kill chain page does. When you shorten a slide, keep "per CrowdStrike", "about" and "moderate-confidence" with the number they qualify. Your copy still credits csoh.org, so a hedge dropped in an edit reads as the site's claim as well as yours; saying which slides you changed keeps the two apart.

Check the date on the cover

Breach pages are rewritten when new disclosures land, and a deck reflects its page as of the date on its cover. Before you present, compare that date with the kill chain page. If the page has changed since, trust the page.

Found something a slide gets wrong? Email admin@csoh.org or open an issue on GitHub, naming the deck and the slide number in its footer.

How the decks are made

Each deck starts as a hand-written outline, a plain-text file kept with the site's code on GitHub. Before it can be published, a script checks the outline against its kill chain page: every number written in digits, every ATT&CK technique and every source on a slide or in its notes must appear on the page, and every stage it names must exist there. Background slides are the one exception. They explain a general concept, say nothing about the incident beyond what the page says, and must name their own source instead.

The outline is then rendered into slides, reviewed slide by slide against the page, and shared from Google Slides. The same check runs whenever a kill chain page changes, so an edit that removes a figure a deck quotes fails the site's checks until the outline is fixed.

Quick answers

Are the decks free to use?

Yes. Downloading the PDF, making your own copy and presenting a deck cost nothing. The decks are licensed CC BY-NC 4.0, so you can share and adapt them for non-commercial purposes as long as you credit csoh.org, link the license and say what you changed.

Do I need a Google account?

Only to make an editable copy. The PDF download and the preview on this page work without signing in.

Does the PDF include the speaker notes?

No. The PDF has the slides only, which is why every slide is written to make sense without its notes. To read the notes, make your own copy and open it in Google Slides.

Why does only part of the kill chain list have decks?

Each deck is checked against its kill chain page and reviewed before it is published, so decks arrive in batches, newest kill chains first. This page lists every published deck, and each kill chain page links its own deck once it exists.
