GMO Onamae.com Desktop Cloud 2026

Step-by-step kill chain mapped to MITRE ATT&CK Cloud, sourced from official post-mortems and primary technical analyses.

August to October 2026 Critical Hosted Desktops / VPS

GMO Onamae.com Desktop Cloud - Customer Remote Desktop VPS → Administrator Account and Management Server → Malware Pushed to Customer Desktops → Saved Broker Logins and Redirected PC Drives → Unauthorized Trades and Extortion

GMO Internet, part of GMO Internet Group, ran Onamae.com Desktop Cloud, a hosted Windows desktop (VPS) service used for automated FX trading. On October 20, 2026, after a customer inquiry the day before, GMO confirmed that a third party had planted a malicious program in some customers' server environments, and from 04:00 JST on October 21 it shut down servers on every plan. At a November 2 press conference, as reported by ITmedia's @IT and other outlets, GMO set out the chain: entry through a customer's remote desktop VPS on August 5, an administrator account taken from there (on August 5 in some reports, on August 17 in @IT's), GMO's management server on August 17, and malware distributed through that server to customer virtual machines from September 20 to October 14. Broker logins saved on the desktops were used for unauthorized trades, and because Remote Desktop drive sharing was on by default, files on customers' own PCs were taken too. An extortion email and a leak site followed. Three caveats belong next to the headline. The figure of 26,739 is GMO's count of servers that may have been affected, not of confirmed unauthorized access. GMO has not said how the attacker got in or obtained the administrator account. And GMO has named no attacker or country.

26,739 servers that may have been affected, which GMO says is not a count of confirmed unauthorized access
2+ months from the management server takeover to detection, from GMO's press conference timeline
US$50 million in Bitcoin or Ether demanded by email on October 21, about ¥8 billion by GMO's figure; GMO refused to pay
Threat actor: not named by GMO; Kyodo News reports a likely North Korea-linked group, citing unnamed sources and IP address overlaps

⚡ Initial Access: A Customer's Remote Desktop
01
On August 5 the attacker got into a customer's remote desktop VPS
T1133 - External Remote Services

Onamae.com Desktop Cloud rented out Windows virtual servers that customers reached over Remote Desktop and used to run FX trading tools such as MetaTrader 4 and 5. GMO dated the first intrusion to August 5 in the timeline it gave at its November 2 press conference, as reported by @IT, and placed it in a customer's remote desktop VPS. Hirofumi Yamashita, GMO Internet Group's executive vice president in charge of systems, withheld the method, citing the need to prevent similar attacks and the effect on the investigation.

The attacker claimed on its leak site to have exploited a Remote Desktop Protocol flaw, according to GovInfoSecurity, and GMO has not confirmed that. Asked at the press conference whether AI was used in the attack, group CEO Masatoshi Kumagai said there was no evidence that it was. The T1133 mapping describes the entry point, a remote desktop service exposed to customers, and is not a confirmed technique.

Date: August 5, 2026, from GMO's press conference timeline (@IT)
Entry point: a customer's remote desktop VPS
Method: withheld by GMO
Attacker's claim: a Remote Desktop Protocol flaw, unconfirmed
Remote DesktopVPSUndisclosed MethodT1133
⬆️ Privilege Escalation: An Administrator Account
02
By August 17 the attacker held an administrator account and used it to enter GMO's management server
T1078 - Valid Accounts

Reports of GMO's press conference timeline differ on when the administrator account was taken. ITmedia Business Online, Business+IT and MediaDogs say the attacker took it at the customer VPS on August 5 and got into GMO's management server on August 17. @IT's account dates the theft of the account to August 17, the same day as the management server intrusion. That server is the pivot of the chain: about five weeks later, the malware went out to customer machines through it.

GMO has not said how the account was obtained or what kind of account it was. The attacker's version, which GovInfoSecurity reported from the leak site, is that the VPS network ran as an Active Directory environment that let it move to other VPS servers, and that it intercepted an administrator password exposed when a support engineer connected remotely to a customer's PC. GMO has confirmed none of these claims.

Account taken: August 5 (ITmedia Business Online, Business+IT, MediaDogs) or August 17 (@IT)
Management server entered: August 17, 2026, in every report of GMO's timeline
Reached: an administrator account, then GMO's management server
How: not disclosed by GMO
Attacker's claims: Active Directory across the VPS network; an intercepted support password
Administrator AccountManagement ServerActive Directory (claimed)T1078
🔀 Lateral Movement: The Management Server as a Delivery Channel
03
From September 20 to October 14, malware went out through the management server to customer virtual machines
T1072 - Software Deployment Tools

The management server is how an intrusion that began in a customer VPS reached other customers' machines. In @IT's account of the press conference, GMO said the malware was distributed through the management server to up to 26,739 customer virtual machines. GMO's written notices use 26,739 as the number of servers that may have been affected, and say it is not the number of cases in which unauthorized access or information acquisition has been confirmed. GMO has not named the malware or said how the management server delivered it, so the T1072 mapping rests on its description of the server as the channel.

GMO did not notice for more than two months after August 17. Yamashita said the company's service monitoring had not caught the intrusion and that it would examine what was missing. A customer inquiry on October 19 started the investigation. On October 20 GMO confirmed the malicious program and suspended new sign-ups and the control panel, and from 04:00 JST on October 21 it shut down servers on every plan in sequence.

Window: September 20 to October 14, 2026 (@IT)
Channel: GMO's management server; mechanism not disclosed
Scale: up to 26,739 customer VMs (@IT); in GMO's notices, servers that may have been affected
Detected: October 20, after a customer inquiry on October 19
Containment: every plan shut down from 04:00 JST, October 21
Management PlaneMalware DistributionMissed DetectionT1072
🔑 Credential Access: Logins Saved in Trading Tools
04
Broker logins saved in trading tools on the desktops were taken
T1555 - Credentials from Password Stores

GMO's second and third reports said a third party may have obtained trading account login information and other data stored on the servers, including logins saved in trading tools, and used it illegally. Its first report, on October 21, had said that no leak of customers' personal information and no misuse had been confirmed at that point. By the fourth report, on October 24, GMO had confirmed a security problem in the service's system, unauthorized access to its environment, and that customer information was taken outside the company.

In a notice to brokers that named neither GMO nor any broker, MetaQuotes, which develops MT4 and MT5, said neither the platforms nor the trading servers had been compromised, and that attackers had got into rented servers running trading terminals and traded with the logins saved there, according to Finance Magnates. GMO has not said how the logins were extracted or how data left its network. The T1555 mapping assumes they were read from the trading tools' saved credentials.

Target: logins saved in trading tools such as MT4 and MT5
GMO's wording: may have been obtained and used (second and third reports)
Confirmed: customer information taken outside the company (fourth report)
Not compromised: MT4/MT5 platforms and trading servers, per MetaQuotes
Unknown: extraction method and exfiltration channel
MetaTraderSaved CredentialsT1555
📤 Collection: The PCs Customers Connected From
05
Drive sharing, on by default, exposed files on customers' own PCs
T1039 - Data from Network Shared Drive

When a customer connected, Remote Desktop drive redirection made the drives of that customer's own PC available inside the session on the infected virtual machine. At the press conference GMO named this default as the reason files on customers' local PCs were taken. According to @IT, Yamashita said the setting had been turned on for customer convenience, and that not switching it off as security risks grew was a lapse in risk analysis. Inside the remote session the redirected drives are reachable much like network shares, which is the basis for the T1039 mapping.

The attacker had made the drive redirection claim on its leak site before GMO confirmed it, and threatened a ransomware attack with a deadline of noon on October 27. GMO asked customers to back up, disconnect and power off their PCs before then, and collected PCs from customers who volunteered them for analysis. Its fourteenth report, on October 28, said analysis of those devices had found no traces of malware infection or ransomware attacks so far, and its ninth report had cautioned that results to date did not confirm the safety of every customer's device.

Feature: RDP drive redirection, on by default (GMO, per @IT)
Effect: customers' local drives readable from the infected VM
Stated by GMO at the press conference (per @IT): files on customers' local PCs were taken; its written notices do not say so
Device analysis: no malware or ransomware traces on the PCs GMO examined (fourteenth report)
RDPDrive RedirectionClient PCsT1039
💰 Impact: Trades the Customers Did Not Place
06
From October 21 customers reported trades in their broker accounts that they had not placed
T1078 - Valid Accounts

GMO's timeline records reports from October 21 of unauthorized operations on customers' trading accounts. @IT, reporting the press conference, says the attacker used the stolen account information on the same day as the extortion email to place trades that deliberately produced losses in FX accounts. Japan's Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama said five FX firms had reported affected customers, according to Bloomberg as cited by Finance Magnates. MetaQuotes' notice to brokers describes trades placed with logins saved on rented servers, and GMO's third report says trading account logins stored on its servers may have been obtained and used illegally, which is why this step maps to T1078. T1078 describes the logins; the losses themselves are the impact.

The extortion message threatened continued unauthorized orders against customer accounts at more than 70 securities and FX companies in Japan and abroad (fourth report). GMO asked major exchanges and securities firms to block access coming through the service, forcibly end all active sessions and watch for unauthorized trading. GMO says it will compensate realized losses on trades it confirms were unauthorized, less any refund from the broker, and it began contacting customers about claims on November 6.

First reports: October 21, 2026 (GMO timeline)
FX firms reporting affected customers: 5, per Finance Minister Katayama
Threatened: 70+ securities and FX companies (fourth report)
GMO's request to brokers: block access through the service, end all sessions
Compensation: realized losses on trades GMO confirms as unauthorized
Broker AccountsUnauthorized TradesSession TerminationT1078
📣 Impact: Extortion, a Leak Site and a Bounty
07
The attacker demanded US$50 million, then published what it claimed was customer data on October 27
T1657 - Financial Theft

The extortion email arrived on October 21, the day GMO suspended the service. The demand, as reproduced on Kumagai's blog, asked for US$50 million in Bitcoin or Ether by 17:00 JST that day; GMO's notices give the amount as about ¥8 billion. The fourth report listed the threats: continued manipulation of trading accounts, publication of information about the service and the customer PCs connected to it along with ransomware attacks, and publication of information about GMO's systems. GMO said it would not pay, because paying would not guarantee that the data was deleted or that its misuse stopped. GovInfoSecurity reported that the extortionists later said they would settle for US$1 million, and IBTimes, citing NHK, reported that on October 27 GMO said it would not pay a US$1 million ransom either. GMO's own notices give an amount only for the October 21 demand.

At 12:30 JST on October 27 GMO confirmed that the attacker had published what it claimed was information about GMO and its customers, and @IT reports that one record of stolen data appeared. GMO warned that the site captured information about anyone who visited it. The site's domain was suspended at around 04:00 JST on October 28. Later that day GMO opened a bounty of its own, capped at US$1 million (about ¥150 million) in total, for information leading to the identification and arrest of those responsible. GMO states that the reward is neither a ransom nor a payment for stolen information; it is paid to people who supply information, and the attacker and accomplices are excluded. By November 6 it had received 852 tips.

Kyodo News reported that, according to unnamed sources speaking on November 1, an analysis by GMO found a North Korea-linked group likely responsible, based on traits of the malware and other traces. Kyodo, working with security experts, also found that five IP addresses that reached the claim-of-responsibility site soon after it went online matched addresses previously used by a North Korean group. Cybersecurity sources in the same report said the site carried traces linked to several countries, possibly a false flag. GMO's public notices attribute the attack to no one. Asked at the November 2 press conference about reports that a North Korea-linked group was likely involved, Kumagai said GMO had analyzed the affected servers and reported the data to the appropriate authorities, and that with the investigation under way he could not name a country or say anything else, according to Reuters.

Demand: US$50 million in BTC or ETH, due 17:00 JST October 21 (email of the same day); about ¥8 billion per GMO
GMO's position: will not pay
Leak: confirmed by GMO at 12:30 JST, October 27
Takedown: site domain suspended around 04:00 JST, October 28
GMO bounty: up to US$1 million for tips, separate from the demand

🛡 How to Defend Against This Chain

Turn drive redirection off by default on hosted desktops. GMO said files on customers' own PCs were taken because Remote Desktop drive sharing was on by default, a setting it had kept for convenience. Microsoft documents that the session host policy "Do not allow drive redirection" wins over a connection setting that allows it, because the most restrictive setting applies, and an empty drivestoredirect:s: value turns it off in the connection files you hand out. If you connect to a desktop someone else runs, Microsoft documents a DisableDriveRedirection registry value that blocks redirection from your own Windows PC.
Change broker passwords from a clean device, then end every session. GMO's guidance set the order: change the password from a device not used for FX trading, then disconnect all active sessions, because some brokers' servers keep existing sessions alive for a while after a password change and a third party could keep trading. End the sessions even if the password was already changed, check the trade history, and turn on two-step verification where the broker offers it. Change any password that was stored on the server or saved in a browser too.
Keep credentials that move money off desktops other people administer. Every broker login saved in a trading tool on these machines was within reach of whoever held GMO's management server. Where a broker offers one-time passwords or passkeys, use them, so that a saved password alone is not enough to sign in from somewhere else. According to Finance Magnates, OANDA Securities plans to make passkeys compulsory, and FXTF said it may switch on one-time passwords for accounts that could have been reached through the service.
Watch the management plane as closely as the tenants. GMO's management server was in the attacker's hands from August 17, and malware went out through it for more than three weeks before a customer's question started the investigation. GMO said its monitoring missed this and that it is examining why. Its notices offer no detection advice. Two alerts follow from what it described: an administrator account used from a customer-facing host, and software pushed to tenant machines with no matching change. GMO also decided not to restart the compromised platform and is offering customers a replacement on an independent cloud platform, which avoids trusting a management layer an attacker controlled.
Give penetration test findings an owner above the team, a deadline and a re-test. GMO Cybersecurity by Ierae, a group company, flagged issues in the service in a diagnosis reported on April 24, 2024. Dempa Shimbun and MediaDogs report four rounds of emergency maintenance between April 26 and May 20, 2024, days after the diagnosis, and ITmedia Business Online, Business+IT and Reuters also place them in April and May 2024; @IT prints the same days with the year 2026. GMO said at the press conference that the fixes lacked depth and speed. Per @IT, the remaining work was put off for the development schedule, the same environment was not re-tested afterwards, and with no escalation criteria the findings did not reach Kumagai. GMO has not said whether the issues Ierae found were the ones the attacker used. GMO Internet president Tadashi Ito said the 2024 report did not cover possible effects on users' own devices, according to MediaDogs.
If you run a brokerage, act on the provider as well as the account. GMO asked major exchanges and securities firms to block access coming through the service and forcibly end active sessions. FXTF went further and blocked logins from overseas IP addresses, and MetaQuotes urged brokers to check clients who trade through third-party VPS services. When a hosting provider your customers trade from is breached, block or step up authentication for traffic from it until those customers have reset their credentials.
