0ktapus / Twilio 2022

Step-by-step kill chain mapped to MITRE ATT&CK Cloud, sourced from official post-mortems and primary technical analyses.

August 2022 Critical SaaS / Identity

0ktapus / Twilio - SMS Phishing at Scale → Credentials and Live OTPs Relayed → 130+ Organizations Breached → And the One That Held

In August 2022 a campaign Group-IB named 0ktapus sent SMS messages to employees at scores of companies, impersonating internal IT and pointing at convincing Okta login pages. It worked repeatedly: roughly 9,931 accounts across more than 130 organizations, Twilio among them, and Twilio's compromise then reached downstream customers including Signal. This chain is on the site for a reason none of the others are. Cloudflare received the same messages, on the same day, from the same actor, and at least three employees entered their credentials and their one-time codes into the phishing page. The attack still failed - because Cloudflare required FIDO2 hardware security keys, and a hardware key will not authenticate to a domain it was not registered to, no matter how convincing the page or how cooperative the human. Every other kill chain here ends in a control that was missing, bypassed, or absent. This one ends in a control that simply worked.

130+ organizations, ~9,931 accounts compromised
Cloudflare employees fell for it too - and it did not matter
FIDO2 is origin-bound, so a lookalike domain cannot relay it
Twilio's response: hardware keys for all employees

⚡ Initial Access - A Text Message From "IT"
01
Employees received SMS messages impersonating internal IT, warning of an expired password or a schedule change, linking to a convincing Okta sign-in page on a lookalike domain
T1566.003 - Spearphishing via Service T1656 - Impersonation

SMS was chosen deliberately. It arrives on a phone rather than in a mail client with a decade of filtering behind it, it carries no sender reputation a user can check, and a shortened link looks normal there in a way it would not in email.

The registered domains combined the target company's name with terms like -okta or -sso, and the pages they served were faithful copies of the real Okta login. Group-IB's analysis found the phishing kit had been used against a large list of organizations, with the target's own branding applied per victim.

Nothing here is technically advanced. The sophistication is in the industrialization: one kit, many companies, all hitting at once.

Channel: SMS to employee mobile numbers
Pretext: Expired password, or a schedule change requiring sign-in
Domains: Company name combined with -okta / -sso patterns
Page: A faithful copy of the organization's real Okta login
Why SMS: No mail filtering, no sender reputation, short links look normal
SmishingOkta LookalikePhishing KitT1566.003
🔑 Credential Access - The One-Time Code Was Relayed in Real Time
02
The phishing page captured username, password and the one-time code together, and an operator relayed them to the real login while the code was still valid
T1111 - Multi-Factor Authentication Interception

This is the step that matters, and it is why "we have MFA" is not the end of the sentence. A time-based one-time code is a shared secret with a short life, so an attacker standing between the victim and the real site can simply forward it inside its validity window.

The victim sees a login that appears to succeed. The attacker holds a real session.

Every phishable factor fails the same way here - SMS codes, authenticator app codes, and push approvals, because all of them are things the user can be induced to hand over or approve. The 0ktapus kit reportedly relayed to a Telegram channel so operators could act on codes immediately, which is what makes the technique scale past one careful attacker.

Captured: Username, password, and the live one-time code
Mechanism: Real-time relay to the genuine login inside the code's validity window
Victim experience: A login that appears to work
Factors defeated: SMS codes, TOTP app codes, push approval - anything the user can pass on
Scale enabler: Captured credentials relayed to operators in real time
OTP RelayAdversary in the MiddleMFA BypassT1111
🌊 Impact - Twilio, and Then Twilio's Customers
03
Roughly 9,931 accounts across 130+ organizations were compromised; at Twilio the access reached customer data, and through Twilio it reached Signal users
T1199 - Trusted Relationship

Group-IB's investigation put the campaign at more than 130 organizations and close to ten thousand accounts, heavily concentrated in companies using Okta - hence the name.

Twilio's compromise is the one with the longest tail, because Twilio provides SMS and authentication infrastructure to other companies: the actor reached data for a number of customer accounts, and through that, phone numbers and SMS verification codes for roughly 1,900 Signal users. That is an identity provider chain running two levels deep, and it prefigures exactly what the Okta support system breach would do a year later.

Twilio also later disclosed a second, earlier intrusion by the same actor in June 2022, found during the follow-up investigation.

Campaign scale: 130+ organizations, ~9,931 accounts (Group-IB)
Twilio: Customer account data reached
Downstream: ~1,900 Signal users - phone numbers and SMS verification codes
Also found: An earlier June 2022 intrusion by the same actor, discovered afterwards
Structure: An authentication provider compromised, then its customers
Group-IBTwilioSignalDownstream ImpactT1199
🔐 The Control That Held - Origin-Bound Hardware Keys
04
Cloudflare employees received the same SMS and at least three entered credentials and codes into the phishing page - and the attack failed anyway, because the second factor was a FIDO2 hardware key

Cloudflare's published account is unusually useful because it does not claim its people were smarter. They were phished, they fell for it, and they typed their credentials into the attacker's page.

What stopped the intrusion was that the attacker then could not complete the login: a FIDO2 security key performs a cryptographic challenge scoped to the origin it was registered against, so a key enrolled for cloudflareokta.com's real domain will not respond to a request from a lookalike. There is no code for the user to read out, nothing to approve, and nothing to relay.

The human failure was total and the technical control absorbed it completely. That is what a good control looks like - one that does not require the person to be having a good day. Cloudflare also noted the hardware keys were mandatory and issued to every employee, which matters because a control with exceptions is a control with a way in.

What happened: Cloudflare staff received the same SMS; at least three entered credentials and codes
Why it failed: FIDO2 keys perform an origin-bound cryptographic challenge
Nothing to relay: No code is displayed, nothing is approved by the user
Coverage: Hardware keys mandatory and issued to all employees, with no exceptions
The point: The control did not depend on anyone spotting the phish
FIDO2Origin BindingControl WorkedCloudflare

🛡 How to Defend Against This Chain

Deploy phishing-resistant MFA, and understand precisely why it is different. This is the clearest evidence on this site that the recommendation is not theoretical. FIDO2 keys and passkeys bind the credential to the origin, so a lookalike domain gets no valid response - which means the attack fails even when the user cooperates fully with the attacker. SMS codes, TOTP and push approval all failed in this same campaign at 130 other organizations. If you take one thing from the whole library, take this.
Make it mandatory and universal, because exceptions are the attack surface. Cloudflare's keys covered every employee. A rollout that exempts contractors, executives, service accounts or "just the legacy app" leaves the exact door the campaign is looking for, and 0ktapus was hitting many companies simultaneously precisely to find the ones with gaps. Budget for spare keys and a hardware-key-based recovery path so nobody needs a phishable fallback.
Do not treat SMS as a low-risk channel for workforce communications. The messages worked partly because employees are conditioned to receive legitimate IT messages by text. Establish and publicise a single channel for IT communications, tell staff that IT will never ask them to sign in from a link, and make reporting frictionless - Cloudflare's employees reported it quickly, which is how the response started while the login attempts were still happening.
Alert on newly registered lookalike domains before the campaign starts. The kit relied on domains combining the company name with -okta or -sso. Certificate transparency monitoring and domain registration feeds surface these within hours of registration, which is usually before the first message is sent. This is cheap and it buys you the initiative.
Restrict access by device posture as well as identity, so a stolen session is not enough. Cloudflare also used mutual TLS device certificates on internal applications, meaning even a valid credential from an unknown machine did not reach anything. Pair phishing-resistant MFA with device-bound access for privileged systems - that combination survives both credential theft and session theft.
Assume the campaign is broader than you, and share. One kit hit 130+ organizations. Cloudflare and Twilio both published detailed accounts including indicators, which is how other targets found their own exposure. Consume that reporting, and publish your own when you can - this chain is teachable only because two companies chose to be specific about being attacked.
